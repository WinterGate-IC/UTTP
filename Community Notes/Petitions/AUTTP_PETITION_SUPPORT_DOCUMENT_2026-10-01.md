## THE PROBLEM

Spam bots — including those operated by UTTP and affiliated networks — are ruining the online experience for millions of people.

They flood social media platforms, forums, discussion boards, and email inboxes with unsolicited messages. They exploit online communities, making it nearly impossible to have genuine interactions without interference. Statistics show that **millions of these spam activities happen every day**, wasting countless hours of time and disrupting focus for creators, workers, and everyday users.

The overwhelming presence of these bots does more than frustrate. It compromises digital security. They pave the way for more serious threats like **phishing scams and malware attacks**. People click suspicious links, waste time cleaning up the mess, and lose trust in the platforms they use.

---

## THE PETITION'S DEMANDS

The petition calls on tech companies and online platforms to take concrete, measurable action:

### 1. Advanced AI-Driven Spam Detection

Platforms must develop and deploy **sophisticated AI-driven spam detection** that can identify and block spam bots before they reach users. This is not a future technology — it exists. The platforms simply choose not to deploy it at scale.

### 2. Stricter Account Verification

Platforms must implement **stricter account verification processes** that make it harder for bot networks to mass-create accounts. This includes:
- Phone verification for new accounts
- Rate limiting on account creation
- Behavioral analysis to detect automated sign-ups

### 3. Effective Reporting Mechanisms

Platforms must provide **effective reporting mechanisms** that actually work. When users report spam bots, the reports should be reviewed by humans, not just automated systems. Coordinated harassment campaigns should be detected and blocked as a pattern, not just as individual incidents.

### 4. Regular Algorithm Updates

Platforms must **regularly update their algorithms** to adapt to evolving threats. Spam bot operators change tactics constantly. Platform detection systems must evolve at the same pace — or faster.

## WHY THIS MATTERS

### 1. The Documentation Gap is Real

For years, victims of coordinated spam and harassment campaigns had **nowhere to file evidence**. Screenshots were scattered in DMs. Accounts were deleted. Threads were buried. Every victim had a piece of the picture and no way to combine it.

This petition is part of closing that gap. It creates a **public record of demand** — a signal to platforms that users are watching and demanding action.

### 2. Spam Bots Are Not Harmless

The petition makes clear what many dismiss: spam bots are not just annoying. They are:
- **Gateways to phishing** — users click suspicious links
- **Gateways to malware** — users download malicious files
- **Tools of harassment** — coordinated campaigns target individuals
- **Tools of disinformation** — bots amplify false narratives

Dismissing them as "just spam" ignores their role as the entry point for more serious harm.

### 3. Platforms Have the Tools — They Just Need Pressure

Every technology the petition demands — AI spam detection, behavioral analysis, mass-account detection — **already exists**. Platforms like YouTube, X, and Discord use variations of these tools for other purposes. The problem is not technical. The problem is **prioritization**.

This petition applies pressure. It tells platforms: **we see what you're not doing, and we demand better**.

### 4. Every Signature is a Signal

Right now, the petition has **4 signatures**. That's small. But that's the point. This is the beginning. If you sign, you become part of the first wave. If you share, you bring others.

**Petitions with 1,000+ supporters are 5x more likely to win.** That's not a statistic. That's a roadmap. Every signature moves the number closer.
