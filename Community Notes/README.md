# TO THOSE WHO WERE HARRASSED

## A document on why the UTTP archive exists, and who it was built for

---

## FOREWORD

This document is not about UTTP.

It is about the people who were on the receiving end of UTTP and never got an answer.

If you were raided, doxed, threatened, extorted, mass-reported, or harassed by accounts that called themselves UTTP — this is written for you.

---

## WHAT YOU WERE TOLD

When it happened, you were probably told one of these things:

- "Just block them."
- "Don't feed the trolls."
- "It's just the internet."
- "Report it and move on."
- "There's nothing we can do."
- "They'll get bored."

You reported it. You blocked them. You moved on — or tried to. And nothing happened. The accounts came back. The harassment continued. The platform did nothing. The authorities had nothing to work with. And you were left with the feeling that you were alone against something that had no shape, no name, and no consequence.

That feeling was not wrong. It was accurate.

---

## WHAT WAS ACTUALLY MISSING

Here is the thing nobody told you.

You were not losing to a stronger enemy. You were losing to a **documentation gap**.

The reason nothing happened was not that UTTP was untouchable. It was that **no one had assembled the evidence in a form that could be acted on.**

- Platforms can only ban accounts they can confirm belong to a coordinated network.
- Law enforcement can only open cases that come with sourced, timestamped evidence.
- Researchers can only write about what's already been documented somewhere.

None of that existed. Screenshots were scattered in DMs. Accounts got deleted. Threads got buried. Every victim had a piece of the picture and no way to combine it.

You were not ignored because your experience didn't matter. You were ignored because there was nowhere for it to land.

---

## WHAT THE ARCHIVE DOES

The UTTP archive was built to close that gap.

It exists for one reason: **to give the evidence a place to live that cannot be deleted, buried, or dismissed.**

When a claim goes into the archive, it has to meet a standard:

- It needs a source.
- It needs a timestamp.
- It needs an archived link where possible.
- It needs to be separated from claims that are still alleged.
- It cannot contain private information that would put anyone at further risk.

If a claim cannot meet that standard, it does not go in. That rule exists to protect the archive from being dismissed — and to protect the people whose experiences are being documented.

**This is not a target list. This is not a dox. This is not a call to action.**

It is a record. And it was built so that when you say "this happened to me," there is something on the other end that proves you were not imagining it.

---

## WHY IT WAS BUILT THIS WAY

Most attempts to "expose" groups like UTTP fail for the same reason: they are written in anger and they are written without standards. They accuse without proof. They mix verified and unverified. They name people who should not be named. They get taken down, or they get dismissed, or they get used as evidence that the accusers are just as bad as the accused.

This archive was built differently, on purpose.

- **No unauthorized access.** Everything in it was either public or donated.
- **No doxxing.** Public handles only. No addresses, no employers, no family.
- **No unverified accusations.** Everything is labeled. If it can't be proven, it says so.
- **No calls to action.** The archive points people to NCMEC, FBI IC3, and Haven Watch — not to retaliation.

The reason for those rules is simple: **an archive that can be dismissed helps no one.** The only way this document serves the people it was built for is if it survives scrutiny. So it was built to survive.

---

## WHAT YOU WERE OWED

You were owed an answer to the question: "Am I the only one this has happened to?"

You were owed an answer to the question: "Is anyone actually keeping track of this?"

You were owed an answer to the question: "Is anyone going to do something?"

The archive does not answer all three. What it does is answer the first two, and it gives the third one a place to start.

- You were not the only one. The network was documented.
- Someone was keeping track. The record is public.
- Something can be done. The evidence exists in a form that law enforcement and platforms can act on.

That is not the same as justice. But it is the first time those three answers have been true at the same time.

---

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN

This archive does not mean your harassment will be undone.
It does not mean the people who harassed you will be arrested.
It does not mean platforms will suddenly start enforcing their rules consistently.
It does not mean you will get closure.

Anyone claiming otherwise is lying to you.

What the archive means is narrower and more honest: **you are no longer alone in having to prove what happened to you.** The burden that was placed on you — to document, to defend, to prove — has been partly lifted. Someone else is carrying part of it now.

That is not the same as being answered. But it is the first step toward being heard.

---

## IF YOU WERE HARRASSED

If your experience is not in the archive and you want it to be, the archive accepts documentation. The rules for submission are published. Everything is reviewed manually before it goes in. Contributors are not asked to take risks. Nobody is asked to infiltrate, entrap, or engage with targets.

If you want action rather than documentation, the archive points you to:

- **NCMEC CyberTipline** — https://www.cybertip.org
- **FBI IC3** — https://www.ic3.gov
- **Haven Watch Foundation** — https://www.havenwatchfoundation.org

If you want to know the archive exists and what is in it, it is public and it is permanent.

You do not have to do anything with it. You do not have to trust it. You do not have to believe it will change anything. You just have to know it is there.

Because for a long time, it wasn't.

---

## WHY IT EXISTS

It exists because the people who were harassed deserved a record of what happened to them.

Not a story. Not a rumor. Not a thread that disappears. A record.

The record is now public. It will stay public. And whatever happens next — whether platforms act, whether law enforcement acts, whether the network collapses under its own weight — the evidence exists.

That is the only thing the archive can promise. And it is the thing it was built to deliver.

---

*This archive was not built to answer for you. It was built so that you are no longer unanswered.*
