## 3. INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION: INSTITUTIONAL SOURCES

The user's account does not stand alone. It is corroborated by government designations, federal prosecutions, and academic research.

### 3.1 U.S. Department of Justice: 764 Is a Criminal Enterprise

The DOJ has prosecuted 764 as a criminal organization engaged in child exploitation, sextortion, and coercion of minors.

In **United States v. Varagiannis and Nepal** (2025), the DOJ charged two leaders of 764 for operating an "international child exploitation enterprise." The affidavit states:

> *"764 is a violent online network that seeks to destroy civilized society through the corruption and exploitation of vulnerable populations, which often include minors. The 764 network's accelerationist goals include social unrest and the downfall of the current world order, including the U.S. Government."*

The DOJ further stated:

> *"These defendants are accused of orchestrating one of the most heinous online child exploitation enterprises we have ever encountered — a network built on terror, abuse, and the deliberate targeting of children."*

In **United States v. Densmore** (2024), Assistant Attorney General Matthew G. Olsen stated:

> *"The defendant in this case, Richard Densmore, is a member of a dangerous network of violent extremists known as 764. This group seeks to do unspeakable harm to children to advance their goals of destroying civilized society, fomenting civil unrest, and ultimately collapsing the U.S. Government."*

Multiple additional DOJ prosecutions confirm the same pattern:
- **Erik Lee Madison** (Maryland): pled guilty to sexual exploitation of a child and cyberstalking, as a member of 764
- **Ryan Catello** (New York): charged with receiving child pornography, alleged member of 764
- **Alexis Aldair Chavez** (Texas): sentenced to 40 years as an administrator of the "8884" network, related to 764

**What this establishes:** 764 is not a "larper" group. It is a federally prosecuted criminal enterprise. Membership is a federal crime.

### 3.2 Institute for Strategic Dialogue: 764 and O9A

The ISD, a leading counter-extremism research organization, published a comprehensive explainer on 764 in March 2025. It states:

> *"764 is a network of online groups that engage in sextortion and the glorification of violence. The network, which comprises a constantly shifting landscape of splinter groups and offshoots, forces minor victims to produce Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). They then use that CSAM as leverage to force victims to perform acts of violence, animal abuse, or self-harm."*

Critically, the ISD documents the ideological link:

> *"Court records have also revealed the influence of extremist groups and ideologies on the 764 network, including the Satanist neo-Nazi group The Order of Nine Angles."*

**What this establishes:** The 764–O9A connection is not a WIC invention. It is a documented finding from court records, analyzed by a major research institution.

### 3.3 Combating Terrorism Center at West Point: 764's Tactics

The CTC at West Point published an analysis in June 2026 titled *"Remotely Coerced Violence: 764, The Com Network, and the Hybridization of Threats."* It states:

> *"764 is a network of individuals who seek to normalize/weaponize criminal activities such as animal torture, zoosadism, incest... 764 threat actors manipulate victims into video recording or livestreaming acts of self-harm, real-world violent crimes, and child sexual exploitation."*

**What this establishes:** A U.S. military academic institution has independently analyzed 764 as a security threat and documented its operational model.
